# Emergent Persistence Hierarchy V1 — frozen protocol

## Hypothesis

One online proof-gated amortization policy, given no layer labels or update
frequencies, can produce heterogeneous persistence depths, recursively promote a
verified subgraph, beat cold cumulative cost, exhibit broader damage under deeper
ablation, and respond to a late regime change by revoking only the shallowest
failing structure.

## Frozen mechanism

Stable identity is the digest of a complete authority-outcome profile, never a
surface token. The controller observes verified trace ASTs, records all subgraphs,
and applies one rule to every candidate:

`promote iff accumulated counterfactual savings - genesis - maintenance > 0`

and all immediate dependency nodes are active. Depth is computed afterward from
the dependency DAG. There are no names for action, procedure, representation, or
developer levels and no scheduled update rates.

The 24-world stream, cost law, opaque profiles, and regime shift at world 13 are
committed before authoritative execution. A complete residual identifies the one
profile invalidated at the shift. The kernel may revoke that node and must retain
unaffected dependencies.

## Controls and gates

Compare cold, adaptive, deep ablation, shallow ablation, matched inert state, and
answer memory. Every evaluation, reuse, maintenance, and genesis charge is
included. The five primary gates are emergent heterogeneous hierarchy, cumulative
advantage with falling late marginal cost, recursive promotion, broader deep
ablation damage, and shallowest sufficient regime repair. Sham and answer memory
must not beat cold.

This bounded synthetic qualification cannot establish natural-world hierarchy
formation, open-ended grammar genesis, unbounded recursion, or policy optimality.
