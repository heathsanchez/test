# Persistent invariant amortization v1

## Frozen hypothesis

An unnamed separator that is uneconomic to construct on one encounter can become
advantageous when its verified syntax and behavioral role are retained and reused
across a prospective sequence of unseen non-isomorphic carriers.

The exact level-complete genesis algorithm and 1,405-call cost from the preceding
negative are preserved. Genesis is paid once. Twelve target carriers of sizes 5
through 16 and their opaque order seeds are frozen before execution. Cold gets no
separator state and exhausts all 256 quotient-role bridges. Persistent warm may
evaluate the retained AST and verify the selected bridge, charging both passes.

The primary endpoint is cumulative charged verifier calls. The gate requires a
crossover and lower terminal cumulative cost. At target 7, the ablation condition
loses the retained separator; its marginal cost must return exactly to cold.
Matched sham and answer-memory controls pay the same one-time state charge but
receive no executable separator.

This is a finite amortization test, not evidence for open-ended continuation
discovery, continuation-language genesis, or natural-domain transfer.
